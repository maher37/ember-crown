# The Ember Crown: Story Bible (v0.1)

*Source of truth for plot, characters and dialogue. Every scene Claude writes for the game should be checked against this doc.*

---

## 1. The pitch

On Lantern Night, when the town of Ashford floats paper lanterns down the river for the miners killed in the Collapse ten years ago (31 died, but the town floats only 30: it leaves out the foreman it blamed, Hadric Varrow), every lantern on the water flares red at once. Minutes later a wounded courier staggers into the Drowned Lantern tavern with a stolen letter, and armed men walk in behind him.

The letter is the first thread of a three-way race for the shards of the **Ember Crown**. In the race are a cult of desperate miners, a mercenary company working for the moneylenders who own the town's debt, and the last daughter of a disgraced family who believes the crown can finally make Ashford pay for what it did to her.

All of them are wrong about what the crown is.

**Themes:** what a town owes the people it scapegoats; desperation vs. principle; whether the past can be repaired or only buried.
**Tone:** grounded and warm with a dark undertow. Humor lives in the townsfolk, dread lives in the mountain, and nobody is evil for no reason.

---

## 2. What we're doing better than the classic starter adventure

The user loved how simple and compelling *Lost Mine of Phandelver* is, but wanted stronger hooks and fuller characters. Design rules that follow from that:

| Common weakness in starter adventures | Our fix |
|---|---|
| The hook is a job ("escort a wagon") | **Personal and supernatural from minute one.** The lanterns flare red, a man bleeds out in front of you, and strangers attack you. |
| The villain appears late and has little presence | **Maelis is in the first scene** (the hooded figure) and stays present: she writes the party letters, delivered by a lantern moth during long rests. |
| NPCs are information booths | **Every named NPC has a want, a secret, and something that changes depending on your choices.** |
| Factions are simply "good" or "bad" | **Three-way conflict.** Each faction is right about something. |
| Fights exist for XP | **Most fights have a talk, sneak or bargain alternative**, and many enemies can surrender or flee. |
| The ending is "kill the boss" | **Clue-driven endings.** What you learned decides what you can say in the final scene. |
| Side quests are filler | **Every side quest gives XP plus a Truth, an ally, or a tool** for the final act. |

## 3. Setting

**Ashford** (our own invented town) sits on the **Grayflow river** in the southern foothills of the **Greypeak Mountains**, in the North of Faerûn, where the Greypeaks meet the **Marsh of Chelimber**. It was built on silver dug from the edges of **Ammarindar**, the dwarven kingdom that once filled these mountains and was abandoned centuries ago. It has been dying slowly since the Collapse closed the deep mine. The town owes ruinous debts to the **Coinwright Guild**, a Waterdhavian lending house. Locals are proud, poor, superstitious and funny about it.

Key places:
- The **Drowned Lantern** (tavern and the party's home base)
- **Ashford Hall** (the mayor)
- The **Old Mine** and the sealed Deep Seam
- **Varrow's Mill** (abandoned, said to be haunted)
- The **Grayflow** river

**Further out:** the **Marsh of Chelimber** and the ruined **Abbey of the Morninglord**, a fallen abbey of Lathander on the marsh's edge (Act 2). In the high Greypeaks is **the King's Kiln**, King Varrow's tomb, carved into an abandoned Ammarindar forge-hall (Act 3). Waterdeep, the City of Splendors, lies far to the west; its money reaches even here.

### History: the legend vs. the truth
- **The legend (what everyone believes):** Two hundred years ago the tyrant **King Varrow, the Ember King**, chained the red dragon **Cindrath**, bound her soul into a crown, and ruled the Greypeak with fire. His own court rose up, broke the crown into three shards, and hid them. The Varrow name has been a curse word ever since.
- **The truth (what the party can discover):** Cindrath was dying of age. She *chose* to pour her soul into the crown so she could outlive her body, then let Varrow find it. Whoever wears the whole crown slowly becomes her. Varrow's "tyranny" was Cindrath wearing him. The court that broke the crown were heroes, and his honor guard swore to guard his tomb forever. They still do.

### Calendar (Calendar of Harptos, Faerûn's standard calendar)
- **Lantern Night is Ashford's local custom for the Feast of the Moon**, the festival between Uktar 30 and Nightal 1 when Faerûn honors its dead. The game opens on the Feast of the Moon, **1492 DR**.
- **Act I:** the first tenday of Nightal ("The Drawing Down"), as winter arrives.
- **Act II:** late Nightal into Hammer ("Deepwinter"), climaxing at the **Midwinter** festival (between Hammer 30 and Alturiak 1, 1493 DR). The marsh steaming in midwinter is the story's clearest sign that something is wrong.
- **Act III:** Alturiak ("The Claw of Winter"), 1493 DR.
- **How time moves:** travel legs take hours, a short rest is 1 hour, and a long rest is 8 hours (once per 24 hours). The date shows on the map, camp and journal screens as "3 Nightal, 1492 DR · Evening". Tendays (10-day weeks) have no day names. Story events are gated by progress, not by the clock.
- **Festival beats:** Feast of the Moon (opening), Midwinter (Act II climax). The epilogue returns to the next Feast of the Moon, when the town floats 31 lanterns if Hadric's name was cleared.

### The ticking clock (escalating signs that the mountain is waking)
- **Act 1:** River lanterns flare red. Hot springs bubble up in the Old Mine. Goblins flee *down* the mountain, starving.
- **Act 2:** The Marsh of Chelimber steams in winter. Dead things in the bog rise wherever the heat reaches. Ash falls on Ashford.
- **Act 3:** The Greypeaks glow at night, tremors crack the Hall, and townsfolk start to evacuate.
- **Design note:** the clock is story-gated, not a real-time timer, so resting is never punished (the user chose normal 5e resting rules).

## 4. Factions

### The Hollow Star (the cult)
- **Who:** out-of-work miners, widows of the Collapse, and a handful of true zealots. Their symbol is a hollow eight-pointed star, the crown's outline seen from above.
- **Wants:** to reforge the crown so "the deep forges burn again", meaning work, warmth and dignity for Ashford.
- **Methods:** they pay wages to dig, preach hope, and grow more violent as Act 2's zealots take over.
- **Right about:** the town abandoned these people.
- **Wrong about:** what the crown will do.

### The Grey Company (mercenaries for the Coinwright Guild)
- *Faerûn option:* the Grey Company quietly takes Zhentarim money on the side, which explains its reach and its ruthlessness. Use this only if you want a known faction in the story.
- **Who:** professional sellswords led by **Captain Ruther Kell**, hired by the Guild to "recover Guild property". The Guild claims ownership of everything under the mountain.
- **Wants:** the shards, delivered to Waterdeep. The Guild wants a weapon; Kell wants to be paid and to bring his people home alive.
- **Right about:** the cult is dangerous.
- **Wrong about:** whether anyone should have that crown, the Guild least of all.

### The people of Ashford
- **Want:** survival. Some will help you; some quietly pray the Star succeeds.
- **Their reactions change** depending on whether you expose the mayor and whether you shame or protect the cultist miners.

## 5. Cast

### Maelis Varrow, "the Lamplighter" (antagonist)
- **Age 30.** Scholar, archivist and soft-spoken. Always carries a hooded lantern. Daughter of Hadric Varrow, the foreman blamed for the Collapse.
- **Want:** to wear the crown, relight the deep forges, and make Ashford say out loud that her father was innocent.
- **Secret:** she hears Cindrath in her dreams and believes the voice is her ancestor's, guiding her home.
- **Voice:** precise, warm, a little sad. Never raises her voice. *"I'm not asking you to forgive me. I'm asking you to look at what this town did, and tell me I'm wrong."*
- **Presence plan:**
  - Act 1: the hooded figure in the tavern; reveals herself at the end of the act.
  - Acts 2–3: a lantern-moth letter at each long rest (6–8 short letters, each written so it still makes sense whichever choices you've made).
  - Act 2: one face-to-face parley.
  - Act 3: the finale.
- **Arc:** she can be redeemed, defeated, or lost to Cindrath (see Endings).

### Cindrath (the true threat)
- An ancient red dragon's will inside the shards. She never lies outright; she flatters and offers.
- In the finale she manifests as an **ember-wraith dragon**: a burning half-shape, not a full-strength young red dragon (balance note in §8).

### Brannoc Hale (innkeeper; the party's anchor)
- **Age 55.** Former miner who survived the Collapse. Gruff, generous, funny when he's tired.
- **Want:** to keep his nephew and his inn safe.
- **Secret:** he signed the false statement that blamed Hadric Varrow because the mayor promised his crew's widows a pension. They never got it.
- **Arc:** wary host → father figure → a confession in Act 2 that can turn into public testimony in Act 3.

### Edric Hale (the courier)
- **Age 19.** Brannoc's nephew, a messenger for Ashford Hall. Brave in a reckless, young way.
- **Want:** to matter.
- **Secret:** he stole the cult's letter from a dead courier on the road and was spotted.
- **Arc:** recovers in Act 1, becomes the party's informant, and can be captured in Act 2 if the party ignores him.

### Mayor Oswin Tarrow (Ashford Hall)
- **Age 60.** Affable, exhausted, and genuinely loves the town.
- **Want:** to save Ashford from the Guild's debts by any means.
- **Secret 1:** ten years ago, as a Guild clerk, he falsified the safety report that blamed Hadric Varrow for the Collapse.
- **Secret 2:** he now secretly takes Hollow Star money to keep the Guild's collectors away. The cult's letter is addressed to "our friend in Ashford Hall".
- **Arc:** the Act 2 betrayal. The party can kill him, expose him, or make him confess publicly, which gives one of the three Truths.

### Brother Ulric (Act 1 boss)
- **Age 48.** Collapse survivor and Brannoc's old shift partner. The Hollow Star's foreman.
- **Want:** wages and a future for the Collapse widows.
- **Arc:** if spared and talked down, he defects and returns in Act 3 to show the party a hidden passage. If killed, Brannoc grieves him.

### Sister Venna (Act 2 boss)
- **Age 35.** A widow of the Collapse and a true zealot. Where Maelis wants justice, Venna wants the town to burn.
- **Role:** shows the party what Maelis's movement becomes without her restraint.

### Captain Ruther Kell (Grey Company)
- **Age 45.** Pragmatic and dryly funny. Keeps his word and counts his dead.
- **Want:** to finish the contract and bring his company home.
- **Arc:** a rival in Act 2. In Act 3 he either holds the tomb gate *with* you (if you earned his respect) or arrives at the end to take the crown for the Guild.

### Smaller roles
- **Wix:** a goblin scout the party can capture and befriend. Calls Cindrath "the Old Red" and is the first to say the mountain is "breathing."
- **Grisk Two-Teeth:** a hobgoblin chief, also fleeing the heat. He'll trade the captured miners for food.
- **Old Wynn:** the town herbalist. Her granddaughter joined the Star.
- **Mother Sallow:** a bog hag in the Marsh of Chelimber. She knows what the crown is and trades knowledge for something precious.
- **Ser Ida Marrow:** captain of Varrow's honor guard. Undead, honorable, and furious.
- **Hadric Varrow:** dead before the story begins; appears only in memories and records.
- **Lysa Varrow:** Maelis's mother. Her ghost haunts the mill.

## 6. The three Truths (the clue system behind the endings)

The finale lets you talk Maelis down. **Each Truth you hold adds a dialogue option, and you need at least 2 for the redemption ending.** With all 3, the speech always succeeds; with 2, it's a Persuasion check with advantage.

| Truth | What it proves to Maelis | Where you can get it |
|---|---|---|
| **The Collapse** | Her father was framed; the town's guilt is real, and someone will say it publicly. | Act 1 side quest *Something in the Cellar* (a dead miner's journal) **or** Mayor Tarrow's confession (Act 2) **or** Brannoc's testimony (Act 2–3) |
| **The Crown** | The voice is Cindrath, not her ancestor; wearing the crown means being devoured. | Abbey archives (Act 2 main) **or** Mother Sallow's bargain (Act 2 side) |
| **The Family** | Her mother didn't want vengeance. | Act 1 side quest *The Mill at Night* (Lysa's ghost) **or** Ser Ida Marrow's testimony of the real King Varrow (Act 3 side) |

Because the main path alone only *guarantees* one Truth, **side quests change the ending**, not just your level.

## 7. Act-by-act plot

**Level curve (updated, see Encounters_and_XP.md):** main path only = Act I 1→3, Act II 3→4, Act III 4→5 with the finale at L5. About 80% of side content keeps you about +1 level ahead and reaches the L6 cap during Act III. Each act also has **one optional elite monster** guarding the act's standout magic item (Items.md): Scald the red wyrmling (Act I), the Drowned Abbot (Act II), and Vorrag the Kiln-Smith (Act III). Each act has **7 mandatory fights** (the full list, including the fights added beyond the story beats below, is in Encounters_and_XP.md §4), up to 4 optional side-quest fights, and random travel encounters between areas. Every fight is telegraphed (marked dialogue, visible hostiles, a "Prepare" step). Encounters are grouped into "rest blocks" (a stretch of fights the party is expected to handle between long rests). This is a design guide only: the player rests under normal 5e rules with Short Rest and Long Rest buttons.

### Act I: "Lantern Night" (levels 1→3)

**Hook (first 5 minutes):**
1. Lantern Night at the Drowned Lantern.
2. The lanterns on the river flare red.
3. Edric collapses through the door.
4. The Grey Company's thugs demand "the Guild's letter."

**Rest block A: the tavern**
1. **The brawl** (L1, nonlethal). Fists, chairs, a thrown bottle. Knockouts use the 5e "Knocking a Creature Out" rule. Win, and Brannoc trusts you. Either way, Edric gasps out "the old mine… they're digging for it."
2. Then the dialogue scene with Brannoc (the mockup), the courier, and the hooded stranger (Maelis, who watches and leaves without a word).

**Rest block B: the road and the warren**
3. **Road ambush** (L2). Starving goblins. Capture **Wix** for the first "the mountain is breathing" warning. Mercy is remembered.
4. **The goblin warren** (L2). **Grisk** holds kidnapped miners. Fight, sneak, or trade food. *Twist:* the rescued "miners" wear the hollow star and beg you not to tell their families. They dig for the cult because it pays.

**Rest block C: the Old Mine**
5. **The sunken shrine** (L3, act boss). **Brother Ulric** and animated skeletons of Varrow's old miners guard the first shard. Ulric can be talked down mid-fight (Persuasion or Insight, easier with Brannoc's name).
- **The shard:** it whispers. Holding it grants fire resistance and a voice in your dreams.

**Act I ending:** Maelis waits at the mine mouth, unarmed, lantern in hand. She explains her father, the Collapse and her vision, and asks for the shard.
- **Refuse:** she leaves peacefully. *"Then we'll talk again, when you've seen more of this town."*
- **Agree:** she takes it. Act 2 then becomes a reclaiming mission (small branch: same encounters, different dialogue).

Either way, she tells you the Grey Company is already marching to the Marsh of Chelimber for the second shard.

**Side quests (4 per act, multi-part; full parts, fights and XP in Encounters_and_XP.md):**
- **The Goblin Who Stayed:** spare Wix on the road, settle his pie-thieving with Brannoc, and recover his clan's luck-stone. *Wix joins as the joke-telling camp companion.*
- **Something in the Cellar:** giant rats in Brannoc's cellar lead to an old tunnel and a dead miner's journal. *Gives the Collapse Truth.*
- **The Herbalist's Granddaughter:** Old Wynn's granddaughter has joined the Star. Find her in the woods (wolves). Bring her home or let her choose. *Gives healing potions and an informant in the cult.*
- **The Mill at Night:** shadows haunt Varrow's Mill. Lay Lysa Varrow's ghost to rest. *Gives the Family Truth.*

### Act II: "The Hollow Star" (levels 3→4)

**Hook:** the Marsh of Chelimber is steaming in midwinter, and the Grey Company and the Hollow Star both have a head start.

**Rest block D: the crossing**
1. **The toll bridge** (L3). The Grey Company holds it. You can fight, bribe, bluff, or parley with **Captain Kell**. If you parley, he respects you, which matters in Act 3.
2. **Drowned Marrowmere** (L3). A bog village where the heat has raised the dead (zombies, ghouls). Ghoul paralysis teaches the party to respect conditions.

**Rest block E: Ashford, then the abbey**
3. **Betrayal** (L4). Back in Ashford, Mayor Tarrow's guards try to seize the shard (or the party, if they gave it to Maelis). Clues from Act 1 (the letter's "friend in Ashford Hall", Edric's information) let you expose him *before* the ambush. That makes the fight easier, or avoids it entirely. Tarrow can confess (*Collapse Truth*). Brannoc, hearing it, makes his own confession.
4. **The abbey undercroft** (L4, act boss). **Sister Venna**, cult fanatics and an ogre guard the second shard.
   - Nearby archives hold the abbey founder's account of the crown (*Crown Truth*).
   - **Reveal:** the crown is Cindrath's soul-vessel, and Maelis doesn't know.
   - **Cliffhanger:** a lantern moth arrives with Maelis's letter. She has the third shard's location and is going to the King's Kiln. She is going to put it on.

**Side quests:**
- **Mother Sallow's Bargain:** the hag knows the crown's truth. She wants something precious: a memory, a name, or ten years of someone's life (choice, with consequences). *Gives the Crown Truth.*
- **Deserters' Bounty:** a bounty on "bandits" who turn out to be Grey Company deserters. Free them, and Kell owes you; turn them in for gold.
- **The Fallen Paladin's Shield:** restore the abbey founder's shield. *Gives a fire-resistance shield that matters against Cindrath.*
- **Edric's Errand:** Edric shadows the mayor's runner, the party steals the Hall ledger, and Venna's enforcers ambush Edric. *Exposes Tarrow early, shrinking the Betrayal fight.*

**Maelis parley (Act 2 midpoint):** the party camps, and she appears in person. It's an honest conversation. If the party holds the Crown Truth, she doesn't believe it *yet*, but it plants doubt (dialogue flag).

### Act III: "The King's Kiln" (levels 4→5; L6 for completionists)

**Hook:** ash falls on Ashford, the Hall cracks in a tremor, and townsfolk evacuate. Brannoc refuses to leave his inn.

**Rest block F: the tomb**
1. **The Kiln Gate** (L4). Animated guardians and traps. Arcana and Investigation solve the gate puzzle. **If Kell respects you,** his company holds the gate against the cult's rearguard, and this fight gets smaller.
2. **Hall of the Honor Guard** (L5). Wights of Varrow's guard led by **Ser Ida Marrow**. If you hold the Crown Truth, Ida *stands aside*; she can be talked to rather than fought.
3. **The Star's Last Stand** (L5). Cult fanatics, the last of Venna's zealots, and fire-warped miners. **If you spared Ulric,** he leads you through a hidden passage and you skip half of this fight.
4. **The Crowning** (L5 main path; L6 for completionists). Maelis stands in the burning heart of the mountain with all three shards, the crown half-forged and Cindrath rising.

**Side quests:**
- **The Cold Forge:** relight Varrow's ancient forge with a shard (resisting the whisper) to quench a weapon. *Gives a +1 weapon of the player's choice (the anti-dragon blade is the Act III elite's reward; see Items.md).*
- **Ser Ida's Unfinished Oath:** help the honor guard captain find her king's remains. *Gives the Family Truth (her account of the real Varrow) if you missed it, and she fights beside you at the finale.*
- **Last Boats from Ashford:** escort the evacuation convoy (hell hounds born of the mountain's heat), then Brannoc's choice: leave the inn or defend it. *Decides the town's epilogue.*
- **Wix's Homecoming:** Wix's clan in the upper caves is threatened by a hill giant. *Gives a back way into the Kiln.*

## 8. The finale and endings

**Balance note:** a full young red dragon (challenge rating 10) is too strong for level-6 heroes. Cindrath manifests as an **ember-wraith**: a reduced-HP stat block, bound to a ritual circle, that weakens as the shards are struck. The finale is about Deadly at L5 and Hard at L6 (see Encounters_and_XP.md); tune it with the balance simulator.

| Ending | How you get it | Epilogue |
|---|---|---|
| **The Lamp Lit (best)** | 2+ Truths and a successful speech. Maelis takes the crown off and breaks it herself. Cindrath dies screaming in the stone. | The mountain cools into hot springs. Ashford becomes a spa town (a bittersweet, funny epilogue: Brannoc's inn is suddenly fashionable). Tarrow's confession clears Hadric Varrow's name; a lantern is floated for him. |
| **The Lamp Snuffed** | You defeat Maelis, then the ember-wraith. | The mountain quiets and Ashford survives, but no one ever learns the truth about the Varrows unless you tell them. Maelis's last letter is found on her body. |
| **The Ember Queen** | Maelis completes the crown (you fail or hesitate). Cindrath is reborn in her and you must fight her. | Darker victory: you kill what Maelis became. |
| **The Ember Crown (evil)** | You take the crown yourself. | Brief "tyrant" epilogue: the mountain obeys you, and you no longer remember your name. |
| **The Guild's Prize** | You hand the shards to Kell. | Waterdeep's moneylenders gain a weapon. An ominous epilogue, and a hook for a sequel. |

Epilogue slides also report on Brannoc, Edric, Ulric, Wynn's granddaughter, Kell, Tarrow and Wix, based on flags.

## 9. The origin character: Corin Varrow (premade, optional)

**Who:** Maelis's younger brother. **Human Sorcerer, Draconic Bloodline (red dragon ancestry, fire)**, so his magic literally *is* the dragon's ember in the Varrow blood. Age 24, with amber eyes like his mother's.
- At 14 he watched the town spit on his father's grave. He left on a river barge and grew up a street mage and sellsword downriver, going by "Corin Ash."
- Charming, guarded, funny to cover the anger. He loves his sister and hasn't spoken to her in eight years.

**Why he's here:** a letter from Maelis, the first in eight years: *"Come home for Lantern Night. I've found a way to make them sorry."* He arrives on the evening barge and is in the Drowned Lantern when Edric falls through the door.

**What changes when you play as Corin**
- **Unique opening:** a short barge scene, his sister's letter, and Brannoc recognizing him: *"You've your mother's eyes, Varrow boy."* Some townsfolk refuse to serve him.
- **The shards respond to him:** they glow when he's near and say his name. Corin gets **[Corin]** dialogue options in most major scenes.
- **The Ember meter (0–5):** at each long rest Cindrath offers Corin a dream bargain. Accepting gives a real boon (stronger fire spells, a free casting, resistance) and **raises Ember**.
  - At Ember 5 the finale offers Cindrath a new host: Corin himself.
  - The party can pull him back with the Truths and his sister's help.
- **Personal beats:**
  - Act 1: the mill ghost is *his* mother; a unique scene.
  - End of Act 1: meeting Maelis is a reunion, not a parley. She asks him to stand with her.
  - Act 2: Tarrow's confession is about *his* father. Corin can choose public justice or private revenge.
  - Act 3: Corin can put the crown on *himself* to keep it from Maelis.

**Corin's unique endings**
- **The Last Varrow:** Corin wears the crown just long enough to break it from the inside. He survives but loses his sorcery. In the epilogue the siblings run the reopened mill together.
- **The Ember Heir (tragic):** Ember 5, nobody reaches him, and Cindrath takes Corin. The party must fight him.
- **Brother and Sister:** a joint speech, Corin plus 1 Truth, counts as 2 Truths for the redemption ending. Playing the origin character makes the best ending a little easier.

**Playing without Corin:** the story is fully complete. Maelis's backstory comes from Brannoc, the mill ghost, Tarrow and the lantern-moth letters. Corin is never mentioned except as "the brother who left," which plants a seed for a future sequel.

## 10. Dialogue rules (decided)

- **Skill checks use the party member with the best bonus** for that skill, and the UI says who rolls ("Insight DC 12 · Thorn +3").
- **Exception: character-specific options** use that character only, shown with a tag:
  - `[Corin]` origin lines
  - `[Cleric]` / `[Paladin]` Religion insights
  - `[Dwarf]` stonework sense
  - `[Background: Soldier]`, `[Rogue]` thieves' cant, etc.
- **Every check fails forward.** Failure changes the story; it never blocks it.
- **No more than 5 choices on screen.** One should always be a plain question and one a way to leave.
- **NPCs remember** (flags): mercy shown, lies told, who you exposed.

## 11. Writing guide (for Claude when writing scenes)

- Every NPC wants something in every scene, even if it's just for you to leave.
- At most 3 sentences per NPC line before a player choice. Let players *ask* for exposition instead of dumping it on them.
- Show the town's humor: gallows jokes, nicknames, petty feuds.
- The villain is always sympathetic and never a cartoon. Maelis is right about the past and wrong about the future.
- Foreshadow each reveal at least twice before it lands (for example, the "friend in Ashford Hall" letter and Tarrow's nervous kindness).
- **Set in the Forgotten Realms (personal use only).** Use real Faerûn geography (Greypeaks, Grayflow, Marsh of Chelimber, Waterdeep), real gods (townsfolk swear by Tymora, the cleric serves Lathander, miners pray to Moradin's old shrines) and light real history (Ammarindar). Keep our own characters, towns and plot; don't copy from published adventures. Monsters and rules come from the PHB/DMG/MM.
- If this game is ever shared, swap the Faerûn names back to invented ones (a simple find-and-replace list lives in RPG_Game_Plan.md).

4. Lantern-moth letters: 6–8 total. Write them early, since they carry the villain.
