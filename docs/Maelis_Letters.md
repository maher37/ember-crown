# The Lantern-Moth Letters (v0.1)

*Maelis Varrow's letters to the party, delivered by a lantern moth at camp. Each arrives at the **first long rest after its trigger**. The camp scene shows the moth landing on the party's lantern while Wix tries, and fails, to catch it. Letters are saved in the Journal under "Letters." Bracketed lines are **conditional** on story flags.*

**Writing rules for these:**
- Maelis never threatens and never begs.
- Each letter gives the player one new true fact, one feeling she didn't mean to share, and one small thing the player can act on.
- Her voice slowly changes as Cindrath works on her: warm and precise early, more grandly certain later, then frightened and honest at the end.

**Optional mechanic (recommended):**
- After reading a letter, the player may **write a short reply** (3 choices: kind, honest, hard).
- Kind or honest replies add **+1 Trust** with Maelis.
- At **3+ Trust**, the finale persuasion DC drops by 2. This makes the letters a quiet second path toward the best ending, alongside the three Truths.

*Already in Corin's pack when the game starts.*

> Corin,
>
> Come home for Lantern Night. I've found a way to make them sorry.
>
> Not with knives, before you ask. With the truth, which is worse for them and better for us.
>
> I've kept your room at the mill. It still leaks.
>
> — Mae

## Letter 1: "Thirty Lanterns"
*Trigger: Act I ends (after meeting Maelis at the mine mouth).*

> To the ones who came up out of the mine,
>
> The moth will wait while you read. It's a lamp-moth from the abbey's old gardens. They fly to the light they were raised on, and I raised this one on mine. Give it a drop of oil before you send it back, if you're the sending kind.
>
> You asked me nothing at the mine mouth. I noticed. Most people have a great deal to say to a Varrow, and none of it is a question. So let me answer the one you didn't ask.
>
> Ten years ago this tenday, the Deep Seam came down on thirty-one men. My father was their foreman. By morning the town had decided the Collapse was his fault, and by spring, that it was his blood's fault. On Lantern Night they float a light for every miner they lost.
>
> They have floated thirty, every year, for ten years. I have counted.
>
> [IF Ulric spared] Thank you for Ulric. He carried me on his shoulders at the harvest fair when I was six. He won't tell you that, so I am.
> [IF Ulric killed] Ulric is dead. You'd like to believe he was a fanatic, I think. He was a foreman with forty mouths to feed and a hymn he only half believed. I'll carry him with the others.
> [IF party gave her the shard] You gave me the shard without a fight. I don't know yet whether that was trust or tiredness. I'll try to deserve the first.
>
> I'm not writing to ask for anything. You'll hear a great many stories about me in Ashford. I'd like one of them to be mine.
>
> — M.V.
>
> [CORIN] P.S. You've grown into Father's shoulders. I didn't say so at the mine because I didn't trust my voice.

## Letter 2: "The Ledger"
*Trigger: first long rest on the road to the Marsh of Chelimber (Act II).*

> To the ones crossing the Grayflow,
>
> Captain Kell will tell you I'm a thief. In fairness, the Coinwright Guild tells him to.
>
> Here is a thing about Ashford that nobody says aloud: it doesn't belong to Ashford. The mine, the mill, the bridge, the ground under Brannoc's inn were all pledged to a lending house in Waterdeep the winter after the Collapse. The town needed coin to bury its dead, and the Guild was very generous at nine parts in ten. Every year since, the interest has eaten a little more of the town. The Guild doesn't want the shards because they're dangerous. It wants them because they're the only thing under that mountain it doesn't already own.
>
> My father kept a ledger: every shift, every name, every rotten timber the Guild wouldn't pay to replace. The Guild's men took it from our house the morning after. I've spent ten years looking for the copy he made, and I found it. That's why I'm doing this the way I'm doing it.
>
> I don't want Ashford to burn. I want it to wake one morning and find the forges warm, the debt turned to ash, and the name on the ledger's last page read aloud in the square.
>
> [IF the missing miners were rescued] Ulric says you brought the Tullow boys up out of the warren. The Hall will thank the Grey Company for it. I'm telling you so that somebody thanks you.
> [IF Wix travels with the party] I hear you've a goblin following you about. Feed it. Things that are fed stay loyal; it's the one rule the Guild never learned.
>
> You'll pass Marrowmere on the way to the abbey. Don't go in at night.
>
> — M.V.

## Letter 3: "Marrowmere"
*Trigger: after the Marrowmere chapel fight.*

> I told you not to go in at night.
>
> I owe you the rest of it. Marrowmere is my doing. Not the way the town will tell it; I raised nothing. But the first shard woke when I lifted it, and its warmth runs down the old dwarf-roads into the marsh. The marsh has a great many dead in it who were content to stay down until they were warm.
>
> I knew the heat would come. I didn't know it would come this fast, or that it would come *there*.
>
> There is a voice I've been following. I believe it's my family's: the first Varrow, the one the songs lie about. It speaks in the forge-cant my father used to swear in. Lately it sounds pleased in a way I don't like.
>
> When I was small I used to hope someone would stop me before I did something unforgivable, like breaking a window or stealing a pie. Nobody ever did, and I learned that hoping to be stopped is just a way of not deciding. So I have decided.
>
> [IF the party told her Lysa's words at the mill] You told me what Mother said, at the kitchen table. I read it back to myself every night now. I can't decide whether it's a kindness or a knife.
>
> But I am sorry about Marrowmere. Burn what's left, if you can. The dead should be cold.
>
> — M.V.
>
> [CORIN] P.S. You were always the one who climbed down the well to fetch the ball. Don't climb down into this one.

## Letter 4: "His Handwriting"
*Trigger: after Mayor Tarrow's fate is decided (Act II).*

> [IF Tarrow confessed publicly] He said it out loud, in the Hall, in front of people. I wasn't there. For ten years I've imagined that moment in every room in Ashford, and in every version I was there. I wasn't. You did that, not me. I don't know yet what to do with being grateful to you.
> [IF Tarrow killed] You've killed the only man who could have said it. I understand why; I wanted to, most nights. But a dead man can't stand in the square, and now it's only my word against the town's, and my word is a Varrow's.
> [IF Tarrow spared but silent] He'll go to Waterdeep, I suppose, and be sorry in a comfortable room.
>
> Oswin Tarrow taught me my letters. Did you know? Before the Collapse he was the Guild's clerk, and on market days he sat on the Hall steps teaching the miners' children to read off the price boards. I was his best pupil. It was his handwriting on the safety report. I'd have known it anywhere.
>
> Brannoc signed it too. I know he did. Tell him I don't blame him; he had widows to think of. Tell him I'd like to blame him, and can't.
>
> — M.V.

## Letter 5: "Burnt Honey-Bread"
*Trigger: Act II ends (after the abbey undercroft, around Midwinter).*

> Happy Midwinter.
>
> My mother baked honey-bread every Midwinter and burned the bottoms on purpose. She said it kept the cold out. I don't know why I'm telling you that.
>
> Venna wanted to burn the Hall with the Guild's men inside it. I didn't tell her to. I want you to know I didn't tell her to. There are people following me now who don't want what I want; they want what I'm *angry* about, and those turn out not to be the same thing. A movement is a fire. You can light it. You can't hold it.
>
> [IF party holds the Crown Truth] You've been in the archives, so you'll tell me the voice is the dragon. I have thought of that, at three in the morning, more often than you'd believe. And then it says something only my father could have known, and I think: what kind of dragon remembers how a man took his tea?
> [ELSE] The voice says you'll try to stop me at the Kiln. It says it fondly, the way you'd talk about the weather.
>
> [IF the party took any of Cindrath's gifts] I've watched the rings on your palms widen, in my dreams. Be careful what you take from it. It gave me a great deal, and I have paid for every gift.
>
> The last shard is in the King's Kiln, under Varrow's own feet. I'm going to put the crown on. When the forges light, Ashford will be warm and free, and it will know my father's name.
>
> If I'm wrong, I'd rather it be you who comes.
>
> — M.V.

## Letter 6: "Ash"
*Trigger: first long rest in Act III (after Ashford begins to evacuate).*

> It's snowing ash on Ashford. I can see it from the pass: the whole valley grey, as if the town has been put away under a dust sheet.
>
> I didn't want the children to be frightened. I keep coming back to that, as if it were the important part. Perhaps it is.
>
> Make Brannoc leave. He won't. He never left anything in his life: not the mine, not the inn, not my father's friendship, not even after he signed. If he stays, sit with him a while. He likes to be argued with. It makes him feel loved.
>
> [IF Wix is a companion] Your goblin left a pie on a rock at the pass for me. It had a face drawn in the crust. I ate it. Please don't tell him; I think it was a test, and I'd like to have passed.
>
> The mountain hums all night now. I sleep a little and dream of being enormous. It isn't unpleasant.
>
> That is what frightens me.
>
> — M.V.

## Letter 7: "Bring a Lantern"
*Trigger: the long rest before the Crowning (the last rest before the finale).*

> This is the last moth. I've no oil left to raise another.
>
> Tomorrow I'll stand in the Kiln's heart with three pieces of a crown and a voice that has stopped sounding like my father and started sounding like me. If you've come to fight, come to fight. I won't hold it against you. I'd do the same.
>
> But bring a lantern. On Lantern Night the town floats a light for each of the thirty-one, and every year they float thirty. Whatever happens tomorrow, I'd like someone to carry one in for him. His name was Hadric Varrow. He was a careful man. He replaced his own timbers when the Guild wouldn't, and that is what he was doing, down there, when the roof came in.
>
> I've written his name thirty-one times in the margin of this letter, the way I used to practice my letters on the Hall steps. I don't know why.
>
> Come before the bells. After the bells, I don't know who will be there to meet you.
>
> — Maelis
>
> [IF party carries Lysa's Locket] P.S. If you have my mother's locket, wear it where I can see it. I'd like to see it once more, whoever I am by then.
> [CORIN] P.S. Corin. Whatever I say tomorrow, it's not me if it calls you "boy." Father never did.

*(Design note: the "boy" line gives Corin's player a concrete tell in the finale. Cindrath calls him "boy," and a [Corin] option lets him name it.)*

*Arrives a season later, in the epilogue.*

> They floated thirty-one this year.
>
> The springs are warm all the way down to the river now. Brannoc charges outsiders a silver to sit in them and calls it "a cure." It cures nothing. They pay anyway. I've started keeping his ledger.
