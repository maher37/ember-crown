# Story review: what Baldur's Gate does, and what The Ember Crown could borrow (Nov 21)

*Written after the owner's playtest note: "going through this story, it's really not that compelling."
Plot summaries of the Baldur's Gate games are from memory and in our own words. Anything marked
[inferred] is my judgement, not a fact. Nothing here changes the story yet: the owner picks what to
adopt, then Story_Bible.md is updated in the same PR (CLAUDE.md: ask before changing a decision).*

---

## 1. What makes each Baldur's Gate compelling

### Baldur's Gate (1998): a mystery about *you*
- **The hook is personal.** The foster-father who raised you is killed in the first minutes by a huge
  armored stranger who wanted *you*. The whole game is "who was that, and why me?"
- **A mystery you piece together.** An iron shortage is wrecking the Sword Coast; letters and orders
  found on enemies point to the next place, and the plot assembles from clues the player reads.
- **Pressure that follows you.** Bounty hunters turn up on the road because there's a price on your
  head. You're never just a tourist.
- **The reveal reframes everything.** You learn what you are (a child of a dead god) and that the
  villain is your half-brother. The last act is personal.

### Baldur's Gate II (2000): a villain obsessed with you, and companions who matter
- **A brutal, intimate opening.** You wake in a cell being experimented on. The villain has taken
  something from you, and the first big goal (rescue your sister) is simple and emotional.
- **The villain is a tragedy.** He was wronged and exiled; you understand him even while you hate him.
- **Companions are the heart.** Each has a personal quest, a past, opinions about your choices,
  banter with each other, and (for some) a romance. Players remember Minsc and Jaheira, not the
  map.
- **Set pieces.** A city run by vampires, an asylum on an island, the Underdark, a dreamscape inside
  your own soul. Each chapter feels like a different kind of story.

### Baldur's Gate 3 (2023): a ticking clock in your own head, and reactivity everywhere
- **The stakes live in your body.** A parasite in everyone's head will turn them into monsters; every
  rest is a reminder, and the parasite also offers power. Use it, or resist?
- **Companions with secrets.** Every companion is hiding something, reacts to what you do (approval),
  and talks to you at camp at night, where many of the best scenes happen.
- **Hard choices with lasting consequences.** Early on you choose between refugees and the goblins
  attacking them, and it reshapes the next act.
- **A voice in your dreams.** A mysterious figure visits during rests, tempting you. (Our lantern-moth
  letters already do something like this.)
- **Layered villains.** Three conspirators, each with their own scheme; you can talk to them, bargain,
  or turn them against each other.

## 2. Where The Ember Crown stands now [inferred]

**Strong already (keep):**
- A good premise: the Collapse, the 31st lantern, and the scapegoated Varrows.
- A sympathetic villain (Maelis) who writes to you.
- Three Truths that decide the ending.
- Talk-past alternatives to most fights.
- Corin's origin, with real family stakes.

**Why it may feel flat in play:**
1. **The custom party has no personal stake.** Without Corin the heroes are hired help. BG1, BG2 and
   BG3 all make the threat *about the player* in the first ten minutes.
2. **Nobody travels with you.** With a party you build yourself (D2), there's no Minsc or Shadowheart.
   The NPCs (Brannoc, Edric, Tamsin, Ida, Kell, Wix) stay behind in their scenes.
3. **Scenes are short "arrive, read, choose, fight" loops.** There's little build-up, twist or
   aftermath. The reveals arrive as text rather than as moments.
4. **Choices rarely come back.** We track a lot of flags, but few later scenes *say* "because you did
   X". BG3's magic is that the world remembers.
5. **There's no felt clock.** The mountain waking is described, but nothing gets worse on screen as
   you dawdle.
6. **There are few set pieces.** Lantern Night is one. The marsh, the abbey and the Kiln are mostly
   fights in different scenery.

## 3. Recommendations, most impact first

| # | Idea | Borrowed from | What it means for us | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Ember-marked heroes** | BG3's parasite, BG1's bloodline | When the lanterns flare red on Lantern Night, the flare **marks every hero** (a warm spark under the skin). Cindrath can now speak to them; the Hollow Star calls them "the kindled". Each act the mark grows, offering a small power (like Corin's bargains, but for everyone) at a cost. Custom parties get a personal stake from minute one. | M |
| 2 | **Camp companions who talk** | BG2/BG3 companions and camp scenes | Brannoc, Edric, Tamsin, Wix, Ser Ida, Kell (and Ulric, if spared) can **join the camp**, not the fights. After big events, one of them has a short scene at the fire: they react to what you did, tell part of their own story, and sometimes ask for something (a small personal quest). Approval decides who stays and how the epilogue goes. | M–L |
| 3 | **The world remembers** | BG3 reactivity | A pass over every scene adding **callbacks**: Brannoc greets you differently if you spared Ulric; townsfolk mention the brawl; Maelis's letters quote your choices back at you; the Lantern Board posts what you did. Cheap, and it makes choices feel real. | S–M |
| 4 | **One hard choice per act** | BG3's grove vs goblins | Make one fork per act genuinely painful, with consequences in the next act. Act I: the miners' strike vs the Guild's debt collectors. Act II: hand Tarrow to the mob or to the law. Act III: who holds the crown shard. Each changes a later scene and a later fight. | M |
| 5 | **A visible clock** | BG1's pressure, BG3's tadpole | The mountain gets worse as days pass: ash on the map, Ashford's mood changes, travel encounters get tougher, and a few side quests close if you wait too long. Rest any time, but the world moves. | M |
| 6 | **Maelis in person, more often** | BG2's Irenicus | Two or three **face-to-face meetings** before the finale (the mill, the abbey, the pass). Each time she knows what you've done and argues her case. She should feel like the main character of her own story. | S–M |
| 7 | **Set pieces** | BG2's chapters | One memorable, different set piece per act: Act I, defend the tavern on Lantern Night as it burns red; Act II, the drowned village at night with the lanterns rising out of the water; Act III, a **dream inside the crown** where you meet Hadric Varrow (or Cindrath wearing his face). | M each |
| 8 | **A mystery you assemble** | BG1's clue trail | A "Truths board" in the journal: clues you find (journal pages, letters, testimony) pinned up, and connecting enough of them **reveals a Truth** as its own scene, not a line of text. | M |
| 9 | **Shorter, punchier scenes** | All three | Trim long description to 2–3 sentences, end scenes on a question or a cliffhanger, and add more spoken lines from people instead of narration. The new dialogue styling already helps. | S, ongoing |
| 10 | **Party banter on the road** | BG2/BG3 | Short exchanges between *your* heroes while travelling, chosen from their class, background and personality (a new pick in the character creator: ideal, bond and flaw from the PHB backgrounds). | M |

**Adopted (owner, Nov 21):** #1 (every hero is marked), #3 and #6. See D28 in DECISIONS.md.
**Adopted (owner, Nov 22):** #2, at camp only. See D32.

**My suggested first step [inferred]:** #1 (ember-marked heroes) + #3 (the world remembers) + #6
(Maelis in person). Together they fix the biggest problem, "why do I care?", without rebuilding the
game. #2 (camp companions) is the biggest win after that.
